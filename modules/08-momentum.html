Week 5 · Firmware literacy

Firmware, apps & Momentum

Know which capabilities come from the hardware, which come from firmware, and how to recover a working setup.

Goal

Record your current firmware, find its documentation, back up your own lab files, and explain the trade-offs of a community build. Official firmware is a complete starting point for this course. Momentum is optional.

Official firmware and community forks

The official project maintains the device firmware and its documentation. Momentum is a community fork with its own changes and release process. Verify its current feature list in its upstream repository; do not assume a video or screenshot describes your installed version.

Official firmware already supports external applications, custom Sub-GHz presets and external CC1101 modules. Those are not, by themselves, reasons you must switch. A fork may add protocol support, UI customization, or changed restrictions; evaluate the specific feature you need.

Handheld firmware choices — check the selected release
Project Focus and capabilities Decision to make
Official Manufacturer-maintained firmware, core device interfaces, external apps and developer tooling. Use as a documented baseline and recovery reference.
Momentum Asset packs, menu/keybind customization, extended configuration and Bad Keyboard identity options; incorporates community protocol work. Check app API compatibility and the specific feature you need.
Unleashed Additional Sub-GHz protocols and tools, including static-code enumeration, rolling-code operations and removed regional TX restrictions. A decoder or saved rolling-code message does not guarantee replay acceptance. Verify radio limits independently.
RogueMaster Combines a broad selection of community apps and firmware changes, with its own release packages. Review the chosen package and changelog; more bundled apps means more versions to track.

These are handheld firmware projects, not ESP32 board images. None adds absent radio hardware or makes an operation legally authorized. Treat firmware changes as experiments: record the baseline, verify a claimed difference, and test recovery.

Board firmware is a separate choice

The official Wi-Fi Devboard runs its own ESP32-S2 firmware. Black Magic/CMSIS-DAP turns it into a debugger; Marauder enables Wi-Fi experiments; a captive-portal project runs a local AP and web page. Each needs the correct board image and, where applicable, a matching Flipper companion. See Module 18: Wi-Fi Devboard & firmware for the comparison, installation and recovery labs.

Hardware and legal limits

Software cannot add an absent radio or turn Sub-GHz hardware into a Wi-Fi interface. An external module changes the hardware path and possibly the antenna performance. Neither a module nor firmware supplies legal authorization.

Keep regional transmit restrictions enabled for the course. This is an operational choice for the labs, not a substitute for learning how radio configuration works. Presets affect demodulation; frequency, bandwidth, modulation and transmit conditions must be understood together.

Lab: make your setup reproducible

  1. Read the device information and record firmware project, version/build, region and date in your notebook. If on official firmware, stay there for this lab.
  2. Copy a small non-sensitive lab file off the SD card using qFlipper and open it on your computer. Keep private credential files outside the public course repository.
  3. Review the official firmware update and recovery documentation. Locate the recovery steps now, before you need them; do not deliberately corrupt firmware to test them.
  4. Inspect the Sub-GHz configuration and current upstream documentation. Identify a default preset and whether your build supports a user preset file. Leave transmit restrictions unchanged.
  5. Choose a harmless app from the official catalog, inspect its description/source/version requirements, and record why it is useful. Installation is optional; explain how to remove it.
  6. If you later switch firmware, first confirm a backup and recovery path, record the release source, and repeat one known lab as a regression check.

Show that you can do it

  • A version and region record, plus a readable backup of a lab file.
  • One verified stock feature and one verified Momentum difference.
  • A recovery reference and a reasoned firmware choice.
