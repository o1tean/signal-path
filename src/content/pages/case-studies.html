The gate that appears to accept a replay

This is a hypothetical diagnostic example, not a verified incident involving a particular gate. In a counter-based model, a previously unused valid message may be accepted if it falls within the receiver’s forward window. Once consumed, the same message is stale.

A forward window does not make an old counter fresh again. If an identical capture seems to work intermittently, investigate the actual protocol, whether a file contains multiple frames, whether receiver state changed, and whether you are observing the same operation. Do not claim a diagnosis from “works once” alone.

Rejected stale messages do not normally advance the receiver counter. Real products have implementation-specific synchronization and recovery behaviour, and operational fob experiments can cause loss of access. Use the simulator to explore the model; use the manufacturer’s documented process for genuine remote provisioning or repair.

The car fob that won't clone

Symptom
You try to copy your car key fob so you have a spare. The Flipper reads something, but the copy never unlocks the car.
What's happening
Modern car fobs are rolling code too, so the same one-time-password logic applies. Provisioning and immobilizer behaviour depend on the specific vehicle and require its documented service procedure.
What to do
Add a spare the way the carmaker intends: enrol a new fob with the documented programming procedure.

A spare remote is easy to picture as a photocopy. Cars work differently. The fob and the car share a secret that was paired at the factory or by a dealer, and, like the gate above, each press is a throwaway code, so a recording can't stand in for the real thing. Cloning the radio signal was never the mechanism for adding a car remote.

The real path to a spare is enrolling a new fob into the car, which depends on the make and model. Some cars have an owner-accessible programming sequence in the manual; many require dealer or locksmith tooling that talks to the car's computer directly. Your owner's manual (or the dealer) is the authority. The Flipper is a superb tool for understanding what your fob transmits; it isn't a key-cutting machine.

The garage remote that copied perfectly and still works

Symptom
You capture an older garage-door remote, replay it, and it opens the door every single time. Unlike the gate, it never stops.
What's happening
This remote is fixed code same every press. There's no counter to move past.
What to do
Nothing is wrong. Enjoy it, and understand what it means.

Plenty of older openers (and cheap gate remotes) send the identical message on every press, often a simple protocol like Princeton with a 24-bit key. Because the message never changes, a recording of it is just as valid tomorrow as today. That's exactly why fixed-code devices are the perfect first capture in Module 3: they behave predictably and teach the whole capture-save-replay loop on your own hardware.

✓
The flip side of "it always works" Fixed code is convenient and also the weaker design: anything that can hear it can repeat it. If a device you own protects something that matters, its fixed-code behaviour is a nudge to consider a rolling-code upgrade. That's you improving your own security, which is always fair game.

Frequency Analyzer says 433.92, but Read shows nothing

Symptom
The Frequency Analyzer clearly locks onto your remote at 433.92 MHz. But when you switch to Read and press the button, nothing decodes.
What's happening
You have the right frequency but the wrong modulation, or a protocol the decoder doesn't recognise.
What to do
Change the preset; if that fails, capture with Read RAW.

Finding the frequency is only half the job. Read has to decode the signal, and that needs the right modulation preset and a protocol grammar it knows. If your remote speaks FSK and you're listening on an AM preset (or vice versa), the energy is there but the message is gibberish, like tuning to the correct station in the wrong language. Work through it in order:

TryWhy
Confirm the frequency, then set AM650The most common preset; fits most fixed-code remotes.
Try AM270, then FM238 / FM476You may be on the wrong modulation family entirely.
Switch to Read RAWCaptures the pulses even when no decoder recognises the protocol.

A successful RAW capture that never decodes into a named protocol is a normal outcome. It means the Flipper doesn't have that grammar built in. It does not mean you failed. The presets reference lists what each one is for, and Module 9 turns this into a full diagnostic flow.

I read my office badge fine, but the clone won't open the door

Symptom
The Flipper reads your work or hotel badge and shows an ID. You write it to a blank, but the copy doesn't open the door.
What's happening
The door system checks more than the number you can read, and there is a policy layer on top of the technical one.
What to do
Treat access credentials as off-limits unless you administer the system.

Reading an ID off a badge reproduces only part of what the reader validates. Many access systems layer on protections a simple clone can't satisfy: cryptographic challenge-response, rolling counters, anti-cloning checks, or a back-end that flags the same credential appearing in two places. So a copy that reads perfectly can still fail at the door. That is by design.

!
Policy stops you before the technology does Your work or building badge belongs to a system someone else administers. Copying it usually breaks the acceptable-use agreement you signed, even where it isn't a crime. Keep your RFID and NFC practice on tags you bought to experiment with.
